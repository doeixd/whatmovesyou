# Related research and better-grounded alternatives

## 1. Research on the Braverman test itself

- **The questionnaire itself:** I found no peer-reviewed psychometric study of it (reliability, factor structure, or comparison with any biomarker).
- **What Braverman did publish:** EEG/P300 research with Kenneth Blum:
  - P300 latency as a predictor of memory impairment (*Clin EEG Neurosci*, 2003; 1,506 patients)
  - The D2 receptor A1 allele and prolonged P300 (*Pharmacogenetics*, 1994)
  - The "Reward Deficiency Syndrome" model (Blum, Braverman et al., 2000)
- **What those papers support:** the brain-electrical side of his model, not the questionnaire.
- **Candidate-gene findings haven't held up.** Most single-gene findings from that era (DRD2 A1, DRD4, 5-HTTLPR and personality) later failed to replicate or turned out to be mostly publication bias. Munafò et al. (2008) found at most ~3% of variance for one DRD4 variant, and nothing for the commonly cited VNTR.

## 2. Frameworks that tie self-report traits to neurotransmitters and have actual evidence

| Model | Dimensions → proposed system | Evidence | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cloninger TCI** (1987–93) | Novelty Seeking → dopamine; Harm Avoidance → serotonin; Reward Dependence → norepinephrine; Persistence | Mixed support. Some DAT-binding and apomorphine growth-hormone studies back NS↔dopamine. HA↔serotonin is weaker. | The closest well-studied equivalent to Braverman. Licensed instrument. |
| **Fisher Temperament Inventory** (2010–15) | Curious/Energetic → dopamine; Cautious/Social-norm-compliant → serotonin; Analytical/Tough-minded → testosterone; Prosocial/Empathetic → estrogen/oxytocin | 56 items, factor-analysed. Two fMRI studies (PLOS One, 2013): Curious/Energetic correlated with substantia nigra/VTA activity (strongest result). Serotonin link weakest. Convergent validity checked against the Big Five (Frontiers, 2015). | The most direct "modern Braverman." Same four-type format, but actually validated. |
| **DeYoung's Cybernetic Big Five** (2013) | Plasticity (Extraversion + Openness) → dopamine; Stability (Neuroticism−, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness) → serotonin | Theory that brings together PET, pharmacology and genetics findings. Uses validated Big Five / BFAS instruments. | The best-supported current account. Free instruments (IPIP) are available. |
| **Gray's BIS/BAS / r-RST** (Carver & White, 1994; Corr) | BAS (drive, reward, fun seeking) → dopaminergic approach; BIS/FFFS → anxiety/serotonergic-noradrenergic avoidance | Large literature. Reward-task fMRI and dopamine drug effects support BAS↔dopamine. | 24 items, free, widely used. |
| **Depue & Collins** | Agentic extraversion → dopamine; constraint → serotonin | Pharmacological challenge studies | Theoretical. Scored with MPQ scales. |

**The overall picture:**
- **Dopamine** has the strongest links to self-report traits: approach, reward sensitivity, novelty seeking, extraversion/openness.
- **Serotonin** links loosely to constraint/stability and harm avoidance.
- **GABA and acetylcholine** have essentially no validated trait questionnaire. Their best self-report proxies are anxiety/sleep (GABA) and cognition/memory (acetylcholine), and those are symptoms with many causes.

## 3. Validated symptom scales for a Part-2-style "deficiency" section

These measure the symptoms Braverman attributes to deficiencies. They don't measure neurotransmitters, but they are reliable and have established norms.

| Braverman area | Validated scale | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Dopamine deficiency (fatigue, low drive, anhedonia) | **SHAPS** (14 items), **DARS**, **Apathy Evaluation Scale** | Anhedonia and loss of motivation, the symptom domain most tied to dopamine |
| Acetylcholine deficiency (memory, word finding) | **Cognitive Failures Questionnaire**, **PROMIS Cognitive Function** | Subjective cognitive problems |
| GABA deficiency (anxiety, tension, palpitations) | **GAD-7**, **PROMIS Anxiety** | Anxiety |
| Serotonin deficiency (sleep, low mood, rumination) | **PHQ-9**, **ISI** (Insomnia Severity Index), **Ruminative Responses Scale** | Depression, insomnia, rumination |
| Impulsivity (3B) | **UPPS-P** (short form, 20 items) | Five impulsivity facets |

The PHQ-9 includes a suicidality item and needs the same crisis-resource handling noted in [validity.md](validity.md).

## 4. How to improve the questionnaire

1. **Use Likert scales instead of true/false.** A 5-point scale gives more information per item and better reliability.
2. **Reverse-key about a third of the items** so that a habit of agreeing doesn't inflate every score.
3. **Give each scale the same number of items**, or report scores as percentages or z-scores against norms.
4. **Remove items that belong to more than one scale or that point in the wrong direction** (insomnia, sleep, blood pressure), and remove pure physiology items (vein visibility, cholesterol) that self-report can't measure accurately.
5. **Keep trait and state separate**, as Braverman already does. Base the trait part on validated dimensions (BAS/Curious-Energetic for "dopamine", Harm Avoidance/Cautious for "serotonin").
6. **Relabel the results honestly**, e.g. "reward-driven / exploratory profile (linked in research to dopamine)" rather than "dopamine dominant."
7. **Validate it:** run it next to the BFAS/FTI/BIS-BAS, check internal consistency (α ≥ .70) and test-retest reliability, and run a factor analysis.
8. **Don't give supplement dosing from questionnaire scores.**

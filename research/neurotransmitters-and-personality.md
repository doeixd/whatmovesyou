# Modern research: neurotransmitters and personality

What current evidence (roughly 2010–2026) says about linking chemical systems to stable traits, organized by system, then by method, and what it means for a Braverman-style test.

**Bottom line:** Stable traits are linked to neurotransmitter systems only weakly and through many small effects. Dopamine↔approach/novelty seeking and serotonin↔neuroticism/negative emotion have the best support. The effects are small (roughly r ≈ .2–.4 in PET studies, often less in large samples), many findings have failed to replicate, and none of them lets a questionnaire infer someone's neurotransmitter levels.

---

## 1. By system

### Dopamine: approach, reward, exploration (strongest evidence)
- **Theory:** DeYoung (2013) argues dopamine's general role is *exploration*. Value coding maps onto Extraversion and salience coding onto Openness/Intellect. Their shared variance, "Plasticity", is the dopamine-linked meta-trait. This framework accounts for novelty/sensation seeking, impulsivity, achievement drive, creativity and schizotypy.
- **PET, dopamine release:** Leyton et al. (2002, n = healthy men) found that amphetamine-induced ventral striatal dopamine release correlated with Novelty Seeking (Exploratory Excitability) and with drug "wanting."
- **PET, effort:** Treadway et al. (2012, n = 25) found that striatal and vmPFC dopamine responsivity predicted willingness to expend effort for larger rewards. Amphetamine increased that willingness.
- **Receptor availability:** Studies of D2/3 receptors and traits (venturesomeness, impulsivity, detachment) find associations in small samples. Addiction samples show a more robust link: low D2/3 goes with high impulsivity (Lee et al., 2009, methamphetamine).
- **Replication problems:**
  - A Karolinska PET replication (Plavén-Sigray et al., 2018) **failed** to reproduce D1-receptor associations with social and antisocial traits. Bayesian evidence favored the null.
  - Castrellon et al. (2019) found that dopamine measures predicted reward discounting in clinical groups but **not in healthy adults**.
  - Small samples, restricted range and flexible analyses inflate early findings.
- **Genetics:** The DRD4 VNTR was not associated with novelty seeking. The C-521T variant explained at most ~3% of variance and showed publication bias (Munafò et al., 2008). DRD2/DRD3 findings are inconsistent.

### Serotonin: negative emotion, constraint, harm avoidance (moderate evidence)
- **PET, 5-HT2A:** The Copenhagen Cimbi group found higher cortical 5-HT2A binding linked to the *inward* facets of neuroticism (anxiety, depression, self-consciousness, vulnerability). This **replicated** in an independent sample with a different tracer (Høgsted et al., 2023, n = 69, r = .37) and did not depend on cortisol.
- **PET, other targets:** Serotonin transporter binding and neuroticism have shown associations in some studies. **No association** was found between 5-HT4 receptor binding and neuroticism (2025). 5-HT1A and self-transcendence replications have been mostly null. These findings depend on the receptor subtype, so "serotonin level" is too crude a concept.
- **Drug evidence:** Tang et al. (2009, n = 240, RCT) gave paroxetine to people with depression. It reduced neuroticism **6.8×** and increased extraversion **3.5×** as much as placebo *among patients with the same degree of depression improvement*, and change in neuroticism predicted lower relapse. This is some of the best causal evidence that manipulating a transmitter system shifts a trait.
- **Genetics:** 5-HTTLPR has a small but significant link with NEO Neuroticism in one meta-analysis (Sen et al., 2004), while other meta-analyses find it inconsistent. Large GWAS don't point to it as a major factor.
- **Depression theory:** The simple "low serotonin causes depression" idea is poorly supported by direct measures (e.g. Moncrieff et al. 2022 umbrella review; that paper's own conclusions are debated). That makes "serotonin deficiency" symptom checklists especially weak.

### GABA and glutamate: anxiety and excitability (early, mixed evidence)
- **Method:** MR spectroscopy (MRS) can measure GABA and Glx (glutamate + glutamine) in living people. It measures total tissue concentration, not synaptic signaling.
- **Findings:**
  - Anterior cingulate Glx predicts trait anxiety in healthy people.
  - Prefrontal glutamine is associated with neuroticism (Translational Psychiatry, 2019).
  - A 2025 pilot found higher dlPFC GABA+ and insula Glx in social anxiety disorder.
  - Reviews report GABA/glutamate associations with impulsivity and anxiety in both directions.
- **Caveats:** Small samples, differences between scanners and methods, and results that point in both directions. There's no validated "GABA personality." Braverman's "GABA nature = calm, organized, dutiful" isn't supported. If anything, GABA/glutamate research relates to anxiety and excitability, which is closer to his *deficiency* scale.
- **Genetics:** GABRA6 and extraversion was not confirmed by GWAS.

### Acetylcholine: attention and cognition, little trait evidence
- **PET:** One study found higher α4β2* nicotinic receptor availability associated with **higher harm avoidance** (anxiety-proneness). That is roughly the opposite of Braverman's "creative, open, romantic" acetylcholine nature.
- **What acetylcholine is known for:** attention, learning, memory and arousal state. It is not a known basis for openness or creativity.
- **Bottom line:** no evidence supports an "acetylcholine personality type."

### Norepinephrine: arousal and vigilance (theory only)
- The locus coeruleus-norepinephrine system regulates arousal, attention and stress responses. Theory papers propose that high tonic NE goes with neuroticism and hypervigilance, and a balanced tonic/phasic pattern with resilience. This isn't directly tested in humans at the trait level.
- Cloninger's Reward Dependence → NE mapping has weak support.
- Braverman's model leaves NE out entirely.

### Endogenous opioids: social attachment (moderate, replicated in one lab)
- **PET:** Nummenmaa, Tuominen et al. (2015; 2020 follow-up) found μ-opioid receptor availability associated with attachment style. Lower availability went with more **avoidant** attachment, and frontal availability with a tendency to form close relationships.
- **Genetics:** The OPRM1 A118G variant moderates how parenting affects attachment and sociability.
- **Relevance:** For "social warmth/bonding" traits, opioids may matter more than any of Braverman's four.

### Oxytocin: social cognition (largely failed to replicate)
- The early intranasal oxytocin findings on trust and emotion recognition haven't held up. The literature suffers from low power and HARKing (hypothesizing after results are known).
- A 2026 registered report with pooled equivalence testing found **no meaningful effect on trust**.
- Meta-analyses find no significant effect on emotion recognition in neurodevelopmental disorders.
- OXTR gene findings are inconsistent.
- Fisher's "estrogen/oxytocin → prosocial" dimension rests on this weaker base.

### Endocannabinoids
- CNR1 haplotypes were linked to neuroticism and agreeableness (Juhasz et al., n = 1,269), but GWAS didn't confirm it.

## 2. By method: what each can and can't show

| Method | Strength | Limitation for personality work |
|---|---|---|
| **PET receptor/transporter imaging** | Direct in-vivo measurement of specific receptor subtypes | Expensive, so n is usually 20–70. Restricted range, many regions tested, and replications mixed. Measures receptors, not "levels." |
| **PET release challenge** (amphetamine displacement) | Measures dopamine *responsivity* | Small samples, dopamine only |
| **MRS** (GABA, Glx) | Non-invasive, repeatable | Measures tissue totals, not synaptic signaling. Low spatial resolution. Method-dependent. |
| **Drug trials** (SSRIs, amphetamine, oxytocin) | Causal | Drugs have multiple effects. Often done in clinical samples. Oxytocin nasal-spray results don't replicate. |
| **Candidate genes** | Cheap, large n possible | Mostly failed to replicate: DRD4, DRD2, 5-HTTLPR, GABRA6, OXTR, CNR1 |
| **GWAS** | Huge n, robust | Traits are highly polygenic (below) and don't cluster in monoamine genes |
| **Receptor atlases** (Hansen et al., 2022) | 19 receptors/transporters, 9 systems, >1,200 people | Group-average maps. The 2026 follow-up (~700 people) measured individual variability but didn't link it to traits, and can't separate real biological variation from measurement noise. |

### GWAS in more detail
- **Size:** A 2024 *Nature Human Behaviour* meta-GWAS (n = 237k–683k per trait) found:
  - 208 loci for neuroticism
  - 14 for extraversion
  - 7 for openness
  - 3 for agreeableness
  - 2 for conscientiousness
- **Genes highlighted:** CRHR1 (stress hormone receptor), SLC12A5 (KCC2, a chloride transporter that sets whether GABA inhibits), MAPT and STX4 (synaptic vesicle fusion). These are broad synaptic, stress and neurodevelopmental genes, not the classic "dopamine/serotonin genes."
- **Implication:** Each trait reflects thousands of small effects across brain development and synaptic function. No trait comes from one transmitter.

## 3. What this means for the Braverman model

| Braverman claim | Current evidence |
|---|---|
| Four transmitters define four natures | ✗ Traits are continuous, many-dimensional and highly polygenic. Other systems (opioid, NE, glutamate, endocannabinoid) matter too. |
| Dopamine nature = driven, strategic, high energy | ◐ Partly supported: dopamine ↔ approach, novelty seeking, effort for reward. But dopamine links more to *exploration/Plasticity* than to "rational, tough-minded." |
| Acetylcholine nature = creative, intuitive, romantic | ✗ No support. The only cholinergic PET trait finding is higher harm avoidance. |
| GABA nature = stable, dutiful, organized (~50%) | ✗ No support for a trait. GABA/glutamate evidence concerns anxiety and excitability. |
| Serotonin nature = impulsive, thrill-seeking, lives in the moment | ✗ Roughly backwards. Serotonin is linked to *constraint* and lower impulsivity, and 5-HT2A binding to inward neuroticism. Thrill seeking is a dopamine-linked trait. |
| Symptom checklists reveal deficiencies | ✗ Symptoms have many causes. No questionnaire has been validated against transmitter measures. |
| Supplements correct deficiencies based on the score | ✗ No trials link questionnaire scores to supplement response. |

## 4. A research-aligned alternative design

If the app wants to keep a "brain chemistry" framing honestly:

1. **Measure validated trait dimensions** rather than transmitter types. Use the BFAS/IPIP-NEO aspects plus BIS/BAS or UPPS-P:
   - **Exploration / Plasticity** (Extraversion-Enthusiasm and Assertiveness, Openness-Intellect, BAS Fun Seeking / Reward Responsiveness). Research link: *dopamine*.
   - **Negative emotionality / withdrawal** (Neuroticism-Withdrawal: anxiety, depression, vulnerability). Research link: *serotonin 2A binding, SSRI-sensitive*.
   - **Volatility / impulsivity** (Neuroticism-Volatility, UPPS-P urgency). Research link: *serotonin / dopamine interplay*.
   - **Constraint / stability** (Conscientiousness, Agreeableness). Research link: *serotonin* (DeYoung "Stability").
   - **Attachment / social bonding** (ECR-RS avoidance/anxiety). Research link: *μ-opioid*.
   - **Stress arousal / vigilance** (trait anxiety, arousal). Research link: *norepinephrine, glutamate/GABA* (early evidence).
2. **Label results as traits** and put each "linked system" in an evidence box with a strength rating (strong / moderate / preliminary / unsupported).
3. **Keep current symptoms separate** using PHQ-9, GAD-7, ISI and SHAPS, framed as "worth discussing with a clinician," not as deficiencies.
4. **Show results as continuous percentiles** against published norms, not types.
